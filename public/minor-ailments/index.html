Minor ailment prescribing in Penticton

21 minor ailments your Penticton pharmacist can treat

You don’t always need a doctor for everyday health problems. In B.C., pharmacists can assess and prescribe for 21 minor ailments, from UTIs and pink eye to cold sores and allergies. It’s free for B.C. residents when you’re seen in person at the pharmacy.

Check your symptoms

Is your condition on the list?

Open any condition to see the usual signs. If it sounds like what you’re dealing with, book an appointment and your pharmacist will take it from there.

Severe symptoms or an emergency? Call 9-1-1 or go to the nearest emergency department. For health advice any time, call HealthLink BC at 8-1-1.

Skin conditions

Mild acne

Clogged pores that keep breaking out on your face, chest or back.

  • Blackheads or whiteheads
  • Small, red pimples, some with pus

Fungal infections: athlete’s foot, jock itch, ringworm and nail fungus

Fungus thrives on warm, damp skin and in nails. Where it shows up tells you which kind you may have.

  • Athlete’s foot: itchy, scaly or cracked skin that may blister, often between the toes
  • Jock itch: an itchy, burning red rash with a clear edge on the groin or inner thigh
  • Ringworm: a round, itchy red patch with a raised, scaly edge on the neck, body, arms or legs
  • Nail fungus: thick, brittle, discoloured nails, usually starting at the tip

Hives, itching and bug bites (urticaria)

Skin that flares up after a bite, a sting or contact with something that irritates it.

  • Itchy, swollen bumps or blisters
  • A stinging or sharp, burning feeling

Swelling of the face or throat, or trouble breathing, is an emergency. Call 9-1-1.

Impetigo

A contagious skin infection that’s especially common in children.

  • Tiny blisters that leak, then dry into a yellow-brown crust
  • Usually found around the nose and mouth
  • More itchy than painful

Shingles

A painful rash caused when the chickenpox virus wakes up again. Treatment works best when it starts early, so don’t wait.

  • Burning, tingling, numbness or pain in one area, a few days before any rash
  • Painful blisters on one side of your body or face

If the rash is near your eye, get medical care right away.

Skin rashes, eczema and dermatitis

Covers eczema, diaper rash, and contact, allergic and seborrheic dermatitis.

  • Dry, red skin that itches
  • Scaly or patchy areas
  • Cracked skin or blisters that ooze

Allergies, mouth and eye conditions

Allergies and hay fever (allergic rhinitis)

Your nose and eyes reacting to things like pollen, dust or pets, rather than a cold or flu.

  • Sneezing
  • A stuffy nose, or a runny one with clear, watery mucus
  • Itchy eyes, nose or throat

Canker sores (mouth ulcers)

Small sores inside the mouth that tend to come back. They aren’t contagious.

  • Shallow, painful sores on your tongue or the inside of your cheek or lip
  • A white or yellow centre with a red edge

Cold sores

Blisters on the lip caused by a common virus. Treatment works best at the first tingle.

  • Tingling, itching or burning at the edge of your lip
  • A cluster of painful, fluid-filled blisters, usually within a day
  • Blisters that break, crust over and clear up in 7 to 10 days

Pink eye (conjunctivitis)

Irritation or infection of the thin layer covering the white of your eye.

  • One or both eyes look red
  • Watery or thick discharge that’s white, yellow or green
  • Itching, burning or a gritty feeling, like something is in your eye

Severe eye pain or changes in your vision need urgent care.

Oral thrush

A yeast infection inside the mouth.

  • Creamy white patches on your tongue, lips, or the inside or roof of your mouth
  • Patches that look a bit like cottage cheese and can be scraped off
  • A dry mouth or less sense of taste

Stomach and digestive issues

Heartburn and acid reflux (GERD)

Stomach acid moving up where it shouldn’t.

  • A burning feeling that rises from your stomach or lower chest toward your neck
  • Burping, or acid coming up into your mouth

Chest pain with shortness of breath, sweating or pain in your arm or jaw could be a heart attack. Call 9-1-1.

Hemorrhoids

Swollen veins around the anus. Common, and nothing to be embarrassed about.

  • Itching, burning or swelling, with or without pain
  • A lump you can feel around the anus
  • A little bright red blood on the toilet paper or in the bowl

Pinworms (threadworms)

Tiny intestinal worms that spread easily, especially among kids and within households. Some people have no symptoms.

  • An itchy bottom
  • Belly pain

Your pharmacist may check by looking, or by using a piece of tape to pick up eggs.

Upset stomach and indigestion

Discomfort in your upper belly, often during or after a meal.

  • Bloating, nausea or stomach discomfort
  • Feeling full soon after you start eating
  • Heartburn, or burping up gas, acid or food

Urinary and reproductive health

Menstrual cramps and period pain

Pain that comes with your cycle.

  • Cramps or pain in your lower belly
  • Lower back pain before or during your period

Urinary tract infection (UTI)

Pharmacists can assess and treat uncomplicated bladder infections.

  • Pain when you pee
  • Trouble holding it in
  • Urine that’s cloudy or smells bad

If you’re pregnant or male, or your UTI is complicated, please see a physician or nurse practitioner instead.

Vaginal yeast infection (candidiasis)

An overgrowth of yeast in the vagina.

  • Itching, irritation and redness
  • White discharge that looks like cottage cheese

Pain, injuries and quitting smoking

Headaches

The everyday kind, not the kind that comes on suddenly and severely.

  • Mild to moderate pain or tightness across your forehead and the sides of your head
  • No nausea or vomiting

A sudden, severe headache is an emergency. Call 9-1-1.

Nicotine dependence and quitting smoking

Ready to quit cigarettes or other tobacco? Your pharmacist can help you get started.

  • A free 12-week course of nicotine patches, gum or lozenges, covered by BC PharmaCare
  • Prescription options, varenicline or bupropion, also covered by BC PharmaCare

Registering for Fair PharmaCare online or by phone gets you covered right away.

Sprains and strains

Mild to moderate injuries from overdoing it or straining a joint or muscle.

  • Pain, tenderness or swelling
  • Redness or bruising
  • Weak muscles or trouble moving the area normally

Good to know

Questions about seeing a pharmacist

Can a pharmacist prescribe medication in B.C.?

Yes. B.C. pharmacists can assess and prescribe for 21 minor ailments, including uncomplicated UTIs, pink eye, cold sores and allergies. You don’t need to see a doctor first.

Can a pharmacist treat a UTI?

Often, yes. A pharmacist can assess and treat an uncomplicated urinary tract infection. If you’re pregnant or male, or your UTI is complicated, you’ll need to see a physician or nurse practitioner.

How much does a minor ailment appointment cost?

The assessment is free for B.C. residents when it’s done in person at a pharmacy. If you’re prescribed medication, you may pay for it depending on your coverage, such as Fair PharmaCare.

What should I bring to my appointment?

Bring government-issued ID, like your BC Services Card, and your Personal Health Number (PHN).

What if a pharmacist can’t treat my symptoms?

Your pharmacist will tell you and point you to the right care, whether that’s a doctor, nurse practitioner or urgent care. Your visit might also end with self-care advice or an over-the-counter product instead of a prescription.

Is my visit private?

Yes. Pharmacists follow the same confidentiality rules as your family doctor, and we have a private consultation area for your appointment.

How do I book a minor ailment appointment?

Book online through the B.C. government’s booking site or call us at 250-422-4262. For help with online booking, call Service BC at 1-833-882-0022, Monday to Friday, 8 am to 4:30 pm.
